About KOLOSVIT

KOLOSVIT ASBL is an innovative association registered in Luxembourg, where future technologies serve real human needs. We believe that digital education, mental resilience and cultural belonging are not luxuries — they are foundations for a dignified life in a new country.

Founded by Ukrainians living in Europe, KOLOSVIT builds bridges between communities, generations and disciplines. We work at the intersection of culture, science and social care — always led by humanity first.

At least 90% of our resources go directly to our mission: supporting displaced people, developing educational programmes and fostering meaningful cultural connections across Europe.

“We are united by shared values — humanity, knowledge, and the belief that every talent matters. Every person who arrives carrying loss also arrives carrying gifts. Our role is to help those gifts bloom.”

🖥 Digital Education — Autonomous systems, AI literacy and future-skills training.
🧠 Neuro-Resilience — The ARCH programme for cognitive health and stress recovery.
🤝 Social Integration — Cultural events, wellness and community belonging.
🌿 Environment — Ecological responsibility as a civic value.

Training & Simulation

Pilot launched May 2026

Simulation • Digital Learning • Vocational Skills • Training R&D

KOLOSVIT ASBL develops innovative training concepts that combine digital technologies, simulation, practical learning and professional skills development.

Our goal is to help educational institutions, companies and industry partners create new learning opportunities, respond to changing labour-market needs and prepare people for emerging technical and professional roles.

We go beyond conventional digital training. Our approach includes research into professional activities, the design of practical learning pathways, development of specialised training materials and the creation of simulation-based exercises adapted to real working environments.

Areas of Development

1. Drone Simulation & Digital Skills

Simulation-based learning for schools, young people, adult learners and professional development programmes.

Potential activities include:

  • Drone flight simulation and operational awareness.
  • Basic flight principles and flight planning.
  • Navigation, observation and situational awareness.
  • Digital mapping and data collection concepts.
  • Introduction to drone-based inspection.
  • Digital literacy and responsible use of AI.

2. Roofing & Building Envelope Skills

Practical and simulation-supported learning for roofing, roof restoration and building-envelope activities.

Potential training topics include:

  • Roof structures and construction principles.
  • Roofing materials and installation methods.
  • Waterproofing, insulation and drainage systems.
  • Identification and documentation of common roof defects.
  • Work-at-height awareness and safety procedures.
  • Drone-assisted roof inspection and visual documentation.

3. Solar PV Installation & Maintenance

Training concepts connecting construction skills, renewable energy and digital technologies.

Potential topics include:

  • Introduction to photovoltaic systems.
  • Roof assessment before solar installation.
  • Planning and positioning of solar modules.
  • Installation workflow and safety awareness.
  • Basic inspection and maintenance concepts.
  • Drone-assisted visual and thermal inspection.

4. Heat Pumps, HVAC & Building Energy Systems

Development of introductory and practical learning modules for technical occupations related to building energy efficiency.

Potential topics include:

  • Basic principles of heating, ventilation and air conditioning.
  • Heat pump systems and components.
  • Installation workflow and technical documentation.
  • Preventive maintenance and visual inspection.
  • Energy-efficiency awareness.
  • Safety procedures and troubleshooting scenarios.

5. Industrial & Facility Maintenance

Simulation and digital learning concepts for technical maintenance and inspection activities.

Potential topics include:

  • Equipment inspection and preventive maintenance.
  • Identification of visible defects and abnormal conditions.
  • Technical documentation and maintenance checklists.
  • Building and facility inspection.
  • Introduction to sensor-assisted monitoring.
  • Digital reporting and maintenance workflows.

6. Drone-Based Inspection & Monitoring

Practical learning concepts using drones as tools for inspection, documentation and monitoring.

Potential applications include:

  • Roof and facade inspection.
  • Solar installation inspection.
  • Industrial and infrastructure visual surveys.
  • Thermal imaging awareness.
  • Asset documentation and reporting.
  • Inspection planning and safety procedures.

Current pilot: in May 2026 we launched our first pilot project — drone simulator training — holding 6 Liftoff licences. Development partners: LuGus Studios (Belgium) and Velosidron.

Training Research & Development Services

KOLOSVIT ASBL also explores the development of customised training solutions for companies, educational institutions, professional associations and industry partners.

Our R&D-oriented services may include:

  • Research into professional tasks, skills requirements and training needs.
  • Design of occupation-specific learning programmes.
  • Development of professional checklists and standard operating procedures for training purposes.
  • Creation of digital learning materials and interactive modules.
  • Development of simulation scenarios and practical exercises.
  • Skills matrices and competency assessment frameworks.
  • Knowledge tests and practical performance assessment tools.
  • Adaptation of technical documentation into structured learning resources.

We aim to transform professional knowledge and operational requirements into accessible, structured and practical training systems.

Simulation for Industry & Organisational Performance

Simulation is not limited to drone operations or technical occupations. We also explore the potential of scenario-based learning in sectors where attention, decision-making, coordination and operational readiness are essential.

Potential applications include:

  • Financial services and investment operations.
  • Railway and transport operations.
  • Energy and critical infrastructure.
  • Aviation and ground operations.
  • Logistics and industrial environments.
  • Emergency response and team coordination.

These activities can be developed in cooperation with sector specialists and connected with the human-performance methodology of our ARCH initiative.

Regulated industries
Simulation for Regulated Industries (Aviation & Rail)

We do not replace mandatory, regulatory training or licensing standards. Instead, we offer complementary advanced modules focused on Human Factors, Fatigue Awareness and Simulation-Based Resilience.

  • Human Factors & Cognitive Readiness — developing scenario-based exercises to help operators manage cognitive load and decision-making during complex situations.
  • Drone Integration Awareness — as drones become part of airspace and railway infrastructure (inspections, security), we develop simulation scenarios for operators to understand and interact with UAVs safely.
  • Advanced Simulation Scenarios — creating controlled environments to practice emergency coordination and communication under pressure, supplementing existing operational checklists.
  • Customized Checklists & R&D — we can design specific, non-standard operational checklists for new technologies (e.g. drone protocols) that integrate with your existing safety management systems.

CurArtA

In development · pilot programmes in preparation

Recovery • Wellbeing • Creativity

CurArtA is an integrated platform dedicated to human recovery, wellbeing, creativity and social connection.

Based in Luxembourg, CurArtA is an initiative of KOLOSVIT ASBL. It develops programmes that support physical, emotional and social recovery through a combination of restorative practices, natural wellbeing approaches, creative activities and meaningful human connections.

Our programmes are designed for people experiencing prolonged stress, emotional exhaustion, displacement, social isolation, difficult life transitions, or the ongoing demands of caring and helping professions.

Our Areas of Activity

1. Stress Recovery & Human Restoration

  • Recovery programmes following prolonged stress and emotional exhaustion.
  • Rest, deep relaxation and restorative experiences.
  • Practices supporting healthy sleep habits and better rest.
  • Activities aimed at restoring inner balance after challenging life events.
  • Supportive programmes for people affected by displacement and social isolation.

2. Body Recovery & Natural Wellbeing

We bring together natural wellbeing practices that support relaxation and recovery of the body.

  • Gentle body practices, movement, stretching and relaxation.
  • Halotherapy — Restorative relaxation sessions in specialized fine-dispersion salt environments.
  • Magnetic Wellness & Contact-Based Applicators — Wellness use of multi-needle applicators (such as LYAPKO) and magnetic products to support relaxation and body balance.
  • Dead Sea Mud Applications — Mineral-rich applications for body care and relaxation.
  • Apitherapy (Bee Houses & Bee Sleep) — Unique restorative experiences in controlled beehive environments.
  • Other contact-based wellbeing products and natural practices.

3. Recovery for Caregivers & Professionals

  • Recovery programmes for social workers, volunteers, healthcare personnel and people in caring professions.
  • Activities supporting the prevention of emotional overload.
  • Restorative experiences following prolonged emotional and professional demands.
  • Team-based recovery and wellbeing programmes.

4. Creative & Emotional Recovery

  • Art, music, singing, poetry and creative self-expression.
  • Art workshops and creative residencies.
  • Cultural gatherings that help people rebuild social connections.
  • Creative activities as opportunities for expression, reflection and emotional release.

5. Social & Community Recovery

  • Mutual support and cooperation between families.
  • Integration of newcomers and local residents.
  • Shared cooking, cultural gatherings and excursions.
  • Intergenerational activities and initiatives that strengthen social connections.

6. Nature-Based Recovery

  • Walking, hiking and outdoor activities.
  • Nature-based relaxation and restorative experiences.
  • Outdoor recovery programmes and gatherings.
  • Combining nature, movement, creativity and social interaction.

7. Horticultural Wellbeing & Social Gardens

We develop community gardens and social green spaces designed to support wellbeing and recovery in harmony with nature.

  • Fresh Air & Gentle Activity — access to clean air and green environments (especially in the Moselle valley region) offering a restful setting, while gentle gardening provides accessible light physical activity and motor-skill practice.
  • Wellbeing & Stress Reduction — contact with nature, gardening activities and tranquil waterside spaces to help reduce everyday stress and restore a sense of inner balance.
  • Healthy Nutrition & Self-Sufficiency — opportunities to grow organic vegetables, herbs, and fruits, supporting specific dietary needs and promoting a healthy lifestyle.
  • Social Integration — shared ecological projects that unite generations and diverse backgrounds, creating an atmosphere of mutual support, trust, and meaningful community engagement.
Integrated Recovery Programmes

CurArtA brings different approaches together to create thematic programmes adapted to the needs of participants and partner organisations. Examples include:

Stress Reset Weekend
A short restorative programme focused on relaxation, nature, movement and creative activities.
Deep Recovery
A multi-day wellbeing experience combining rest, sleep-supportive routines, gentle movement and natural wellbeing practices.
Caregiver Recovery
Restorative programmes for volunteers and professionals working in demanding environments.
Creative Recovery
Programmes combining art, music, creative expression and social connection.
Our Approach

We believe that recovery is a multidimensional process. Rest, movement, creativity, nature, supportive relationships and meaningful experiences can all contribute to personal wellbeing.

CurArtA develops non-clinical programmes that complement, but do not replace, professional healthcare, psychological support or medical rehabilitation.

Our activities are adapted to different groups and may be developed in cooperation with qualified specialists and partner institutions.

CareterA — Property Intelligence, Care & Community Activation

In development · seeking pilot partners

Property Intelligence, Care & Community Activation

From property care to smarter use of buildings, land and community assets.

CareterA is an initiative developed by KOLOSVIT ASBL to help property owners, municipalities and organisations better understand, monitor, maintain and activate their buildings and land.

We bring together practical property services, digital monitoring, building assessment and new approaches to the use of underutilised spaces. Our goal is not simply to look after properties, but to help identify their condition, potential and possible value for the wider community.

CareterA is being developed step by step — starting with practical property-related services and extending towards municipal asset management and community activation.

1. Property Monitoring & Care

Practical care for buildings and land

We aim to provide practical inspection, monitoring and care solutions for vacant and occupied properties — public, municipal and private.

Detection & Monitoring
Identification of vacant or underused buildings and land, followed by monitoring of their condition and potential use.
Building Inspection
Visual assessment of roofs, attics, basements, façades and other accessible building elements. Technical documentation and condition reports for houses, farms, warehouses, offices and industrial properties.
Drone-Based Inspection
Where appropriate and subject to operational requirements, aerial imaging can support the inspection of roofs, façades, solar installations and hard-to-access areas.
Territory & Land Care
Monitoring and coordination of maintenance for surrounding land, gardens, agricultural plots and outdoor spaces.
House Sitting & Property Stewardship
Temporary presence and property supervision through appropriate agreements, including commodat arrangements and paid services. The purpose is to help owners maintain oversight of properties during periods of absence or non-use.

Some specialised inspection and monitoring services are under development and may be delivered with qualified external partners.

2. Digital Building Passport
Understanding a property's condition and potential

CareterA is developing the concept of a Digital Building Passport — a structured digital profile that can help owners and organisations understand the condition, characteristics and possible future use of a property.

Depending on the building and the available information, the assessment may include:

  • Digital Building Survey — visual documentation and structured property data, including 3D survey options.
  • Building Condition Overview — identification of visible defects, maintenance needs and areas requiring further professional inspection.
  • Roof & Attic Potential — assessment of possible uses, including solar installations and other renovation opportunities.
  • Energy & Renovation Scenarios — preliminary analysis of possible improvement options.
  • Adaptive Reuse Potential — identifying opportunities to give an underused building a new function.
  • Digital Decision Support — bringing property information together to support planning and investment decisions.

The Digital Building Passport is a developing concept. It is intended to support owners and decision-makers, not replace certified technical inspections, engineering assessments or statutory energy audits.

3. Municipal Property & Asset Management

Turning underused assets into opportunities

Municipalities often manage a diverse portfolio of buildings, land and public spaces. Some assets may be underused, awaiting renovation or lacking a clearly defined future purpose.

CareterA aims to help municipalities identify these assets, understand their condition and explore practical opportunities for their use.

Potential areas of cooperation

  • Inventory and digital documentation of municipal buildings and land.
  • Preliminary assessment of property condition and maintenance priorities.
  • Identification of vacant and underutilised municipal assets.
  • Exploration of renovation, reuse and energy-improvement opportunities.
  • Assessment of possible social, cultural, educational and economic uses.
  • Identification of potential partners, operators and project opportunities.

Possible assets include

Former schools and public buildings · Farms and agricultural properties · Historic buildings · Community facilities · Parks and gardens · Public land and outdoor spaces · Unused commercial and industrial premises.

The objective is to help municipalities move from fragmented property information towards a clearer understanding of their assets and the opportunities they offer.

4. From Property Activation to Community Activation — CAP
Community Activation Platform

Buildings are not just assets. They can become places where communities grow.

Property activation can be the starting point for something broader.

An unused building, former school, farm, garden or public space may become a place for education, culture, social activities, wellbeing, local enterprise or community cooperation.

Example: transformation of unused municipal land and parks into community gardens, social agricultural spaces and horticultural wellbeing sites.

This is where CareterA connects with the broader KOLOSVIT Community Activation Platform (CAP) concept. CAP is a developing framework for bringing together municipal assets, local needs, organisations, businesses, associations and project partners.

Rather than treating each initiative as an isolated project, the approach is to identify connections between available spaces, community needs and the people or organisations capable of creating meaningful activities.

Community Needs & Opportunities

ARCH — Human Performance, Operational Readiness & Resilience

In development · pilot phase

Human Performance, Operational Readiness & Resilience

An AI-enabled ecosystem for understanding human performance, strengthening operational readiness and building resilience across organisations, critical infrastructure and communities.

Understanding people. Strengthening performance. Building resilience.

ARCH is an evolving innovation initiative by KOLOSVIT ASBL, Luxembourg, designed to explore and develop integrated approaches to human performance, wellbeing, operational readiness and resilience.

It brings together human-centred technologies, voluntary wellbeing monitoring, data-informed analysis, simulation-based training, cognitive performance development and recovery practices.

ARCH aims to help organisations better understand the human factors that influence performance, decision-making, safety, adaptability and wellbeing — particularly in environments where people work under pressure, manage complex processes or carry significant responsibilities.

From financial institutions and critical infrastructure operators to aviation, railway systems, municipalities, educational institutions and local communities, ARCH is designed as a flexible ecosystem that can be adapted to different operational and social needs.

Our approach recognises that organisational resilience is not determined by technology and infrastructure alone. People, their capabilities, their condition, their ability to make decisions and their capacity to adapt are equally important.

1. The ARCH Approach

From human-state awareness to stronger organisational resilience

ARCH is built around the relationship between human performance, operational environments and organisational resilience. We aim to develop an integrated approach that combines four complementary stages:

OBSERVE
Understand the Human State

Explore relevant indicators that may influence human performance and wellbeing.

Depending on the programme, this may include:

  • Voluntary physiological data from wearable devices and sensors.
  • Self-reported wellbeing, fatigue, stress and perceived workload.
  • Information about working conditions, schedules and operational context.
  • Results of training exercises and simulation scenarios.
  • Individual and team-level feedback.

The objective is to establish a more informed understanding of factors that may affect performance, without turning personal monitoring into employee surveillance.

UNDERSTAND
Analyse Human Factors

Use structured assessment and, where appropriate, AI-assisted analytical tools to identify patterns and trends relevant to human performance.

Potential areas of analysis include:

  • Cognitive workload and concentration.
  • Fatigue-related indicators and recovery patterns.
  • Stress-related factors and perceived pressure.
  • Attention and situational awareness.
  • Adaptability and decision-making under pressure.
  • Team interaction and communication.
  • Organisational conditions that may contribute to excessive workload.

ARCH seeks to translate relevant information into understandable insights that can support individuals, programme coordinators and organisations.

TRAIN
Develop Human Capabilities

Strengthen individual and collective capabilities through practical learning and scenario-based activities.

Potential formats include:

  • Simulation-based training.
  • Decision-making exercises.
  • Cognitive and attention-training activities.
  • Stress-management and self-regulation techniques.
  • Team communication and coordination exercises.
  • Scenario-based learning for complex operational environments.
  • Digital skills, AI literacy and technology-oriented education.

ARCH connects its human-performance methodology with KOLOSVIT's separate Training & Capacity Building initiative.

RECOVER & IMPROVE
Build Sustainable Resilience

Support recovery, self-regulation and continuous improvement.

Depending on the target group and programme, activities may include:

  • Structured recovery sessions.
  • Relaxation and breathing practices.
  • Gentle physical activity and movement.
  • Wellbeing and resilience workshops.
  • Individual self-management strategies.
  • Reassessment and evaluation of programme outcomes.

The purpose is not simply to measure difficulties, but to help people and organisations develop practical ways to respond to them.

3. Technology & Innovation

Connecting Human-Centred Approaches with Emerging Technologies

ARCH explores how appropriate technologies can support a better understanding of human performance and the development of resilience programmes. Technology is not the objective in itself. It is a tool that should serve clearly defined human, organisational and research purposes.

Wearable Technology & Human-State Monitoring

Wearable devices and physiological sensors may provide useful information for selected pilot programmes.

Potential applications include:

  • Heart-rate monitoring.
  • Heart-rate variability analysis where supported by the device and measurement protocol.
  • Activity and recovery-related indicators.
  • Voluntary self-reported wellbeing and fatigue assessments.
  • Comparison of selected indicators before and after training or recovery activities.

ARCH is exploring the use of wearable technologies, including Polar H10, as part of its initial development and pilot activities.

Measurements must be interpreted within their technical and contextual limitations. Individual physiological indicators cannot, on their own, establish a person’s level of stress, fatigue, health or operational fitness.

AI-Assisted Analytics

ARCH aims to explore AI-assisted tools for organising and interpreting relevant information.

Potential functions include:

  • Identification of patterns and trends in voluntarily provided data.
  • Structured analysis of self-assessment results.
  • Aggregation of programme feedback.
  • Support for the evaluation of training and recovery activities.
  • Development of informative reports for programme participants and organisations.
  • Assistance in designing individual and group resilience programmes.

Any use of AI must be purpose-limited, transparent and subject to appropriate human oversight.

Simulation & Digital Environments

Simulation offers opportunities to explore human responses to complex situations in controlled learning environments.

Potential applications include:

  • Scenario-based decision-making.
  • Attention and coordination exercises.
  • Situational-awareness training.
  • Team communication exercises.
  • Digital and technology-related skills development.

Responsible Use

ARCH does not treat. ARCH detects, informs and recommends. It complements — never replaces — healthcare, social services and clinical judgement.

Participation in monitoring is voluntary, and personal monitoring is not intended to become employee surveillance.
